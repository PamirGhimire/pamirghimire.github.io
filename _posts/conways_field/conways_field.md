In software engineering in large organisations, one speaks often about Conway's law. The law claims that the architecture of systems put together by large organisations often mirrors the interfaces that exist in that organisation. The software is 'cut' in a more or less same way the organisation is. And it is futile to fight this tendency of architecture to evolve according to the organisation that creates it - when the two are head to head, the organisation always wins. 

Those of us that have worked in software can perhaps nod and acknowledge that we have felt something of the sort in our lives too. But this force that pulls architecture along organisational fault lines, what's its shape? How can one actually feel it? Is it possible to actually tell when it's in the room with us?

Then you make architectural decisions regarding how to make those changes. These decisions though are never made in vacuum, for they frequently have stakeholders from different teams.
